## Q6: identification is now substantially stronger

The [1976 National Register nomination](https://files.nc.gov/ncdcr/nr/WA0008.pdf), PDF page 7, describes the stored pig by an acorn, two oak leaves, a scalloped blanket, and damaged hind legs. Those carved features are better identifiers than the later coat color. PDF pages 4–7 give the earlier animal inventory; page 14 supplies the lettered arrangement. Lowercase letters denote the center row, uppercase letters the inner row.

The city's public catalogue supplies a direct bridge from that carving to a restoration number:

| Primary record | Date / identifier | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Acorn detail slide](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/photos/12294) | **PPC2019.001.237**, slide dated **9 September 1981** | Catalogue description explicitly identifies two oak leaves and an acorn and transcribes **7A**. The photograph visibly shows the carving. |
| [Pig drawing](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/archives/18630) | **PPC2018.001.095**, drawing itself undated | The drawing is marked **No. 7A** and shows the same scalloped blanket and acorn/leaf ornament. The 2018 accession prefix is not its creation date. |
| [Earlier pig slide](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/photos/12287) | **PPC2019.001.230**, **August 1979** | Identifies the worn pig as 7A; the scan shows the animal off the carousel against a plain background. |
| [Painted pig slide](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/photos/12299) | **PPC2019.001.242**, **March 1982** | Shows the freshly painted black-and-white acorn pig, still identified as 7A. |

Inspect the saved [drawing](sources/raleigh-drawing-pig-7a.jpg), [acorn detail](sources/raleigh-slide-acorn-detail-1981.jpg), and [1982 slide](sources/raleigh-slide-pig-7a-1982.jpg). This identifies the **same carving**, rather than merely another pig.

### Why this still does not certify `g`

The [NCA photograph 023](https://www.carousels.org/psp/PullenPark/Pullen%20Park023.html), dated **28 May 2004**, calls the acorn pig a second-row figure. That supports a center-row placement at that later date. The proposed `g` also resembles a conversion from the number seven to the seventh lowercase position. Neither alone proves where the animal was first inserted.

The earlier inventory already records a different pig at `g`, while `G` holds a wooden horse. There were also aluminum substitutes at `a`, `h`, `H`, `L`, and `N`. Consequently, selecting a letter requires resolving animal movements. One cannot assume the stored pig replaced an aluminum horse or silently replace the pig already at `g`.

A newly located [restoration working map](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/archives/17285), **PPC2018.001.001**, is labelled **Spring 1976**. It has tape, stickers, and handwritten additions over a lettered plan. Its public scans are saved as the [whole map](sources/raleigh-restoration-arrangement-map.jpg), [jumper key](sources/raleigh-arrangement-map-jumper-key.jpg), and [outer key](sources/raleigh-arrangement-map-outer-key.jpg). The catalogue dates the sheet to 1976; that does **not** separately date every later annotation. The available images did not yield a legible, dated 7A-to-letter placement entry.

Additional numbered cat and rabbit drawings were compared with NCA photographs to test the numbering. They do not justify an automatic conversion of an animal identifier to a coordinate: the older carving descriptions and later pairings require individual checks. No complete photographic sequence anchored on a particular fixed chariot was established. The same limitation applies to choosing uppercase `G` merely from an imagined A/B row convention.

**Date qualification:** the dated slides show work on 7A through 1981 and fresh paint in 1982. They do not prove a return by 1980, but they also do not exclude an earlier structural repair or temporary return followed by later paint conservation. The premise is **unverified**, not demonstrated false.

**Evidence still needed:** a legible dated installation register or marked arrangement showing 7A at a lettered position; alternatively, a dated photographic sequence that identifies both chariots and counts the intervening jumper positions. A later photograph alone is insufficient to establish the original insertion.

## Q7: a real unit estimate, with a material date discrepancy

The most useful new primary source is Cy Farley's **31 August 1976 progress report** to the Civic Committee to Restore the Pullen Park Carousel. It reports decisions from the **29 June 1976** meeting. On its first page, the paragraph below decision 1 names Dick Norton of Connecticut, dates his examination to **1971**, and records an **“approximate figure of $400.00 per animal”** at that time.

Proof: [city catalogue record, PPC2024.002.020](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/archives/34906), [public first-page image](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/api/images/98180), and [saved scan](sources/raleigh-progress-report-1976-08-31.jpg). Read the lower half of the page, immediately above decision 2. This is a genuine per-animal estimate, rather than a calculation from a project total.

However, the report does **not** establish a **1975** estimate, nor does that paragraph enumerate stripping, sanding, painting, and rebuilding. The source therefore supports **$400 as a lead**, not the complete answer requested in Q7. A possible date error in the report or in the question cannot be resolved by assuming which is correct.

The 1976 nomination's **over-$100,000 valuation** of the carousel is also not a restoration unit cost. Neither a valuation nor a later final expenditure should be divided by 52 to manufacture an answer.

### Remaining archival lead, now more precisely located

The city catalogue holds [*Merry-Go-Roundup*, vol. 10, no. 1, Spring 1983](https://raleighhrmprogram.catalogaccess.com/archives/20325), **PRCR2018.008.028**, featuring *A Salute to Pullen Park's Dentzel Carousel*. This confirms the earlier handoff's bibliographic lead. Its public catalogue images include **only the front cover, rear cover, and publication page 2**; they do not provide the article's interior text. The membership-protected NCA archive was not bypassed, and nothing was purchased.

The next decisive evidence would be Norton's dated **1975** appraisal/estimate, or the relevant interior pages of the 1983 feature explicitly quoting its date, unit price, and work included. The [Raleigh Fine Arts Society collection, MSS 035](https://catalog.wake.gov/Record/778096), remains another relevant repository lead; the detailed finding aid did not deliver readable content during this review.
