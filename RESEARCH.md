**1. Why Williamsburg fits the photograph**

The input has a pink pig with a turquoise saddle, an orange lower blanket, a red neck ribbon with two bells, and a curly tail. A dark cat and an ostrich are behind it. The white column has a green spiral; the wall has a historical street engraving.

Compare the [official museum cat photograph](https://cwf20siteprodstg.blob.core.windows.net/prod/images/Sidewalks_to_Rooftops-_Outdoor_Folk_Art_1.width-500.jpg), saved [locally](sources/williamsburg-cat.jpg). Its cat and column match the input. A [Williamsburg Tourism Council photograph](https://assets.simpleviewinc.com/simpleview/image/upload/c_limit%2Cq_75%2Cw_1200/v1/crm/williamsburgva/Listings-43-18-_BEDB09EA-F0CB-ED50-CF5BA5FEF21371A0-bedb072ce4a742a_bedb4f34-edc5-353d-3bad9280d9287330.png), saved [locally](sources/williamsburg-tourism-carousel-display.png), shows the broader installation with its camel, pink pig, green spiral column, and historical street backdrop.

Colonial Williamsburg's [current exhibition page](https://www.colonialwilliamsburg.org/discover/historic-area/art-museums-of-colonial-williamsburg/exhibitions/sidewalks-to-rooftops-outdoor-folk-art/) identifies *Sidewalks to Rooftops: Outdoor Folk Art* as on view and illustrates its carousel camel and cat. The [official collections introduction](https://emuseum.colonialwilliamsburg.org/groups) explains the two museums under the Art Museums of Colonial Williamsburg: the Abby Aldrich Rockefeller Folk Art Museum and the DeWitt Wallace Decorative Arts Museum. This also explains the “combined collections” clue.

**2. Why the three companions are cat, camel, and ostrich**

[American Roads, Spring 2016](https://americanroads.net/art_spring2016.htm), in its discussion of the Abby Aldrich Rockefeller Folk Art Museum, explicitly describes the carousel group as “a cat, a camel, an ostrich and a pig.” The page is [saved locally](sources/american-roads-2016.html).

For the requested 2020–2026 context, combine the supplied 2026 photograph (pig, cat, ostrich) with the current official exhibition page (cat and Bactrian camel) and the matching installation photograph. The 2016 article alone does **not** prove an uninterrupted display throughout 2020–2026. Its role is to corroborate the membership of the four-animal group.

**Attribution qualification**

The [official cat record, accession 1969.703.1](https://emuseum.colonialwilliamsburg.org/objects/58638/carousel-figure-cat), names the Dentzel Carousel Company and uses a qualified attribution to Salvatore Cernigliaro. Its expanded label explains that other shop carvers repeated his designs. Therefore the design attribution should not be converted into certainty that Cernigliaro personally carved every figure. A catalogue record for the pictured pig was not located. The artist implied by the missing original Q1 should be checked against the pig's actual exhibit label.

**3. Why Pullen Park fits**

The [Raleigh Fine Arts Society's 2015 newsletter](https://archive.constantcontact.com/fs142/1102316631685/archive/1122369945080.html) attributes Pullen's 52 animals to Cernigliaro. The [National Carousel Association's Pullen census entry](https://carousels.org/USACensus/census.pl?NCANo=150), last updated in 2021, describes an active three-row wooden carousel with four pigs. The [city's current amusement page](https://raleighnc.gov/parks-and-recreation/places/pullen-park-amusements) places the attraction in Raleigh.

The park also matches the more specific later clues: a documented stored pig, a lettered historic arrangement, and the flag-bearing horse/lion pairing. I found no source that separately proves the global superlative about the greatest number of original Cernigliaro pieces. Treat that part of the clue as an unconfirmed premise, not an established statistic.

**4. Why the trailing animal is a lion**

The 1976 nomination identifies outer position XVII as the probable lead horse and XVI as the lion (PDF page 4). [NCA photograph 004](https://www.carousels.org/psp/PullenPark/Pullen%20Park004.html), dated **10 November 2006**, shows the flag horse facing right with the lion immediately behind it in the same outside row. See the [saved photograph](sources/nca-pullen-flag-horse-and-lion.jpg).

This supports **lion**. The primary evidence inspected did not independently state the rumor about counting rotations, so identifying the rumored horse with the lead horse remains an inference.

**5. What is established, and what is missing, about the pig letter**

The 1976 inventory's stored pig has an acorn and two oak leaves (PDF page 7). It lists operating pigs at `g`, `p`, and `P`, and aluminum horses at `a`, `h`, `H`, `L`, and `N` (PDF pages 4–7). The plan is on PDF page 14. The row labels use lowercase for the center row and uppercase for the inner row.

[NCA photograph 023](https://www.carousels.org/psp/PullenPark/Pullen%20Park023.html), dated **28 May 2004**, shows an acorn-decorated pig in the second row. See [the saved photograph](sources/nca-pullen-second-row-pig.jpg). A tentative comparison with the plan suggests lowercase `g` for this later location. However, `g` already had a pig in 1976. A dated restoration arrangement or a complete, anchored photographic sequence is needed to establish whether animals moved and where the stored pig was first reinstalled.

Do **not** treat the photograph as proof of a placement by 1980. Do **not** choose `G`, `h`, or another letter merely because a horse appears in the earlier inventory. No supported final choice is available yet.

**6. What is established, and what is missing, about the 1975 estimate**

The 1976 nomination names Dick Norton, of Horse of a Different Color in Bristol, Connecticut, as an expert who examined and valued the carousel (PDF page 11). That identifies a promising lead, but does not establish who produced the requested 1975 per-animal estimate.

The [1982 reopening clippings in NCA Resource Guide 008](https://carousels.org/CRG/NCA_Carousel_Resource_Guide008.pdf), particularly PDF pages 1–4, report a **1976 whole-project estimate of $60,000** and a **final total of $330,798**. These are different quantities and dates from the requested 1975 amount. Dividing either total by the animal count would not answer the question.

The strongest uncompleted archival lead is [Raleigh Fine Arts Society Collection, MSS 035](https://catalog.wake.gov/Record/778096), at the Olivia Raney Local History Library. Its project files cover the restoration. Its linked [detailed finding aid](https://libraryarchives.wakegov.com/repositories/2/resources/43) returned 403 in both the research tool and browser. The [Merry-Go-Roundup index](https://carousels.org/MGRIndex.html) points to a major Pullen article in **volume 10, number 1 (Spring 1983)**; [online back issues require membership](https://carousels.org/mgronline.html). Neither access limitation establishes the missing amount.
